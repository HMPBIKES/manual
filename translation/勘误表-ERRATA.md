# 《聚焦冥想》原书勘误表 · Focus on Meditation — Errata

**说明（中文）**：以下勘误是本次中译过程中逐句比对原文时发现的，依据 2026 年第一版书稿（Focus_on_Meditation_Final.docx）。条目正文用英文书写，可直接转给作者 Richard Josephson 用于再版修订。分五类：A 结构性问题、B 事实与数目不一致、C 病句与残句、D 拼写与用词、E 标点与引号。定位方式为章节名 + 句子引文。

**Note (English)**: The following errata were collected during a sentence-by-sentence Chinese translation of the first-edition manuscript (2026). Locations are given by section title plus a quoted phrase. Suggested fixes are offered where the intent is clear; items marked "author to decide" need an editorial decision.

---

## A. Structural issues 结构性问题

**A1. Sentence cut off mid-edit.**
Section *Integrating the Entire Process* (first occurrence, chapter *Putting Your Stamp on Your Practice*), last paragraph ends:
> The single most important aspect of building a viable practice is thinking "not-two." By "not two" I mean that everything is dharma practice. We must

The sentence stops at "We must". The parallel section in the *Exposure* chapter carries the full continuation ("We must as dharma practitioners learn to lead low contrast lives…"), which suggests this paragraph was truncated during editing.

**A2. Two sections duplicated almost verbatim.**
*Do Not Try Out Meditation Techniques* and *Integrating the Entire Process* each appear twice — once in *Putting Your Stamp on Your Practice* and again in *Exposure* — with near-identical wording (only minor phrasing differs). Both appear twice in the Table of Contents as well. Unless the repetition is intentional, recommend keeping one copy of each (the *Exposure* versions read as the later revision: they are fuller and fix A1's truncation).

**A3. Chapter title likely missing punctuation.**
*Visualization Communicating Through Meditation* — presumably "Visualization: Communicating Through Meditation" (matching the book's other "title: subtitle" headings).

## B. Factual / numeric inconsistencies 事实与数目不一致

**B1. "All three" vs. four items.**
*Imagination, Innovation, and Creativity*: the paragraph lists **four** things to maintain (a high level of enthusiasm; an unwavering wish to develop increased meditation skills; capacity to absorb and interpret complex philosophical texts; a sense that our practice is continually maturing), then says "If any one of these is missing, we should get it back. If all **three** are missing…" — should be "all four" (or trim the list to three).

**B2. "Five colors" vs. six chakras, with blue twice.**
*Filters*: the text says "the chakras and their **five** colors", then lists **six** positions — "The crown chakra is green, the forehead white, the throat red, the heart blue, the naval yellow, the base of the spine blue" — with blue appearing twice. The Newton demonstration in the same section names five colors (red, green, blue, yellow, purple). The following paragraph adds that the heart "may be blue or black". Recommend reconciling the count and the list. (Also "naval" → "navel".)

**B3. Newton's prism as "validation" of chakra theory — author to decide.**
Same section: "Newton gave a demonstration … which strikingly **validates** the chakra theory of light." Newton's experiment shows that white light decomposes into and recombines from spectral colors; it does not validate claims about body energy centers. Suggest softening to an analogy ("strikingly parallels" / "offers a suggestive analogy to") to stay consistent with the book's stated preference for reason over speculation (*Practical Considerations* chapter).

## C. Garbled or incomplete sentences 病句与残句

**C1.** *Depth*: "The less distinction we see between ordinary thought, the greater the depth of our meditation will be" — the comparison is missing its second term; presumably "between ordinary thought **and meditation**".

**C2.** *Subtle, Sensible, and Logical*: "The time to shift gears is not a choice we make but one that will force itself upon us as we grow and the time to shift is no longer a choice but a necessity." — the clause is stated twice; recommend deleting one half.

**C3.** *Integrating the Entire Process* (second occurrence): "For months or years for months or years, the distinction…" — duplicated phrase.

**C4.** *Predictable, Controlled, Results*: "a good place to start is with an awakened master's of a traditional text" — missing noun; presumably "an awakened master's **commentary on** a traditional text".

**C5.** *Correcting Aberrations*: "Stock traders discussed above that the conservative ones face similar problems." — garbled; suggest "Of the stock traders discussed above, even the conservative ones face similar problems."

**C6.** *Shotgun Meditation*: "The cause of the proclivity **is to try** an assortment of practices hoping that one of them will accomplish the task can usually be found in a failure to delineate…" — suggest "The cause of the proclivity **to try** an assortment of practices … can usually be found in…".

**C7.** *Insight vs Intuition*: "could not possibly been prepared for" → "could not possibly **have** been prepared for".

**C8.** *Insight vs Intuition*: "But Chan masters work in mysterious and I sometimes wonder…" → missing "**ways**".

**C9.** *Insight vs Intuition*: "Insight takes an object and looks at anew" → "looks at **it** anew".

**C10.** *Imagination, Innovation, and Creativity*: "we should maintain **at** a high level of enthusiasm" → delete "at".

**C11.** *Enthusiasm*: "when otherwise we may ended a meditation session" → "may **have** ended".

**C12.** *Practical Considerations*: "There is no reason for a dharma practitioner should not eat simple food" → "There is no reason a dharma practitioner should not eat…".

**C13.** *Practical Considerations*: "it is often difficult resist the urge" → "difficult **to** resist".

**C14.** *Check Your Histogram Graph* (context: greedy trader): "he is willing put his money on the line" → "willing **to** put".

**C15.** *Partition Your Sub-text*: "The wisdom of the Buddha's words when he says, 'the wise man removes the impurities…'" — sentence fragment; suggest "Such is the wisdom of the Buddha's words: …".

**C16.** *Understanding Light*: "our most fulfilling days may not be our happiest. but it may be our best day" — capitalization and agreement; suggest "…may not be our happiest, but they may be our best."

**C17.** *Cold, Neutral, Warm*: "Human being are impossible to absolutely categorize" → "Human **beings** are…".

## E. Punctuation and quotation marks 标点与引号

**E1. Misplaced or unbalanced quotation marks**, e.g.:
- Section title and TOC entry: `The" Scene" and being drawn into it.` → `The "Scene" and…`
- *The "Scene"*: `teacher in the "strict sense of one who…` — opening quote never closed
- *Elements of Composition*: `'magnificent dullness"` — mismatched single/double
- *Nothing is Black and White*: `we are "thinking' about "important matters"` — mismatched
- *Predictable, Controlled, Results*: `a "slick interpretation of a "New Age" teacher,"` — nested quotes tangled
- *Insight vs Intuition*: `such books as "The Blue Cliff Records, "could not…` — closing quote misplaced
- *Processing Your Raw Data*: `'We are more likely to be asked, "What does your job pay?"…'` — stray opening quote

**E2. Double periods** in several places, e.g. *Color and Emotion* ("devotional gestures.."), *Cold, Neutral, Warm* ("tamasic.."), *Do Not Try Out Meditation Techniques* (2nd) ("always is.."), *Shotgun Meditation* ("procedures.."), and similar.

**E3. TOC page-number formatting**: entries run the title into the page number without a separator (e.g. "What is Meditation?1") — likely a generated-TOC artifact to re-render before print.
