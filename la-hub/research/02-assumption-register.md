## 1. A01 · 场地 · Borda 16

**假设：** Riders who work Koreatown, Westlake, USC and DTLA will detour to 768 Ceres Ave (Warehouse District, one to two blocks off Skid Row) for battery swaps 3-7x/week, rentals, returns and service at an SF-like 'share willing to visit' (implicitly 70-90%), so the SF 'sell the vehicle, keep renting the battery' line (300+ of 1,000+ buyers, ~30% attach) becomes LA's recurring revenue engine from a single hub with no geography penalty and no formal swap competitor.

**为什么可能错：** SF geography does not transfer: 261 6th St sits within ~1.5 mi of nearly every SF delivery hotspot, so a swap, warranty swap or return is a 5-10 minute detour. 768 Ceres is ~1-1.7 mi from Little Tokyo / the DTLA core but ~4.3-6 mi from Wilshire/Western-Vermont, ~3.3-3.5 mi from MacArthur Park/Westlake and ~3 mi from USC, across the 110 and the DTLA one-way grid: 18-35 minutes each way at dinner peak, i.e. a 40-60 minute round trip costing 1.5-2.5 orders (~$12-20 gross) plus the swap fee, every swap. LA couriers commute in from South LA, East LA, Pico-Union, the SGV and the Valley, so the depot is off-route for most and home charging beats hub swapping; battery swap is the most distance-sensitive behavior because it requires the most frequent visits. Skid Row adjacency adds reluctance to leave a $3-5k vehicle, walk in, or visit after dark, exactly when swap demand peaks. Zero walk-by visibility also removes the cheapest acquisition channel a bike shop has. SF's own swap stations only launched June 2026, so the 300+ SF battery renters are mostly home-charging rentals and there is no SF evidence yet that swap-hub economics work anywhere. Separately, DoorDash now lists Swobbee (battery-swap subscription, 50% off for 3 months) as a Dasher partner alongside Whizz and Dirwin, and Whizz/Zoomo bundle battery, maintenance and theft cover into the rental, so a platform-steered network of swap cabinets beats a single hub on convenience and the standalone battery upsell disappears for their riders.

**方向：** 若错则 LA 更差  ·  **幅度：** Koreatown share willing to visit 20-40% instead of 70-90%; effective daily-swap catchment shrinks to DTLA core, Arts District, Fashion District and Little Tokyo (40-60% smaller than the target list implies); battery attach falls from ~30% of buyers to 5-15% outside a ~1.5-mile radius; LA year-one subscriber ceiling plausibly 50-100 rather than an SF-scaled 300 and recurring battery revenue roughly one-third of an SF-scaled projection. Since Koreatown is the largest chunk of proposed TAM, SAM drops 30-60% and the recurring (swap/rental) lines drop more than sales; rental-vehicle utilization falls as pickup/return friction rises; Month 6/12 subscriber triggers would be set against the wrong denominator. Theft/vandalism shrink of 1-2 units per month on a 50-100 unit fleet is 12-24%/yr fleet loss before insurance.

**签约前的便宜测试：** Ride Wilshire/Western to 768 Ceres and back on a FLASH / 28 mph moped at 6:30-8pm and 9:30pm on a weekday with a timer; visit the block 9-11pm on a Friday and note conditions; overlay DoorDash/Uber Eats hotspot maps and Yelp/Google restaurant density on 1.5/3/5-mile rings from the site; intercept-survey 30 couriers at K-town clusters (6th/Western, Wilshire/Vermont) on home ZIP, the maximum detour minutes they would accept for a swap at a stated price, whether they would ride to 7th & Ceres weekly and at what hour, or would rather pay Whizz's bundled price; count e-mopeds parked outside DTLA vs K-town restaurants at 7pm; check Swobbee/Popwheels station maps and any LA-specific Dasher perks; ask the landlord and neighbors about break-ins in the last 12 months; get an insurance quote for a 50-vehicle fleet at that address. Build an SF distance-decay curve (attach and churn by 0-1 / 1-2 / 2-4 / 4+ mi from 6th & Folsom) and apply LA target-zone distances to 768 Ceres.

**需要 HMP 内部数据：** SF customer home and work-zone ZIPs vs distance to 261 6th St; battery-rental attach rate over time, swap/exchange frequency per subscriber per week and churn by distance band; share of subscribers living or working more than 2 miles from the shop and whether anyone in SF regularly rides more than 15 minutes to swap; share of visits after 7pm; share of SF battery renters using the June-2026 swap station vs home charging; rental-return and service-visit frequency; average distance riders travel to swap; SF theft/loss rate per vehicle-year; SF share of customers acquired by walk-in; any change in attach or churn after Whizz's DoorDash SF launch.

**可一票否决：** 是  ·  **提出视角：** demand_sizing_method, ramp_and_cac, site_operations_safety, sf_to_la_transferability, competition_and_platforms（合并自 5 条）

> 评审（model_output_sensitivity）：The geography term in SAM and the volume term of the recurring line. Koreatown/Westlake/USC sit 3-6 miles and a 40-60 minute peak round trip from Ceres, so willing-to-visit falls from an implied 70-90% to 20-40% for the largest chunk of TAM (SAM -30-60%); battery attach falls from ~30% to 5-15% outside ~1.5 miles, so the year-one subscriber ceiling is 50-100 rather than an SF-scaled 300 and recurring battery revenue is ~1/3 of projection. Rental utilization also falls with pickup/return friction. A 2x error in the visit-willingness share moves SAM and the swap line roughly 1:1 and mis-sets the denominator of every Month-6/12 subscriber trigger.

> 评审（least_testable_pre_lease）：Whether Koreatown / Westlake / USC riders will repeatedly detour to a Skid-Row-edge warehouse 3-7x/week for swaps is a revealed-preference, repeat-behavior question. The cheap tests only bound it: a timed ride gives the objective detour cost, intercept surveys give stated willingness (unreliable for a 40-60 minute round trip), the SF distance-decay curve has no customers in a 4-6 mile band across a freeway, and a K-town pop-up tests in-neighborhood demand rather than detour-to-Ceres demand. Worse, the swap-hub model itself is unproven even in SF (stations launched June 2026), so there is no base to transfer. It is the largest chunk of proposed TAM and could flip the decision alone; only A16 is less testable, because at least the detour cost here is physically measurable.

> 评审（probability_of_being_wrong）：Koreatown is 4-5 miles from 768 Ceres across the 110; ZIP 90021 has ~2,900 residents, so there is neither local demand nor walk-by. Swapping is the most frequent visit and therefore the most distance-sensitive behavior, and SF's own swap station only opened June 2026, so there is no evidence even in SF that a hub-swap model works at any distance. Tried to argue single hubs work (Whizz/Zoomo run one per city) and failed: they bundle swaps into the rental and 261 6th St is within ~1.5 mi of every SF hotspot, which Ceres is not. Skid Row adjacency adds after-dark reluctance exactly when swap demand peaks. Consequence: Koreatown/Westlake/USC largely fall out of the catchment, battery attach drops to 5-15% outside ~1.5 mi, and the recurring-revenue engine the lease is justified on is about one-third of an SF-scaled projection.

## 2. A02 · 竞争/平台 · Borda 13

**假设：** HMP can hold SF-level whole-vehicle rental pricing in LA (~$45-65/week, ~$195-280/mo) plus a separate battery subscription (implied $50-90/week all-in for a moped) because Whizz only offers pedal e-bikes and its LA 'from $139/mo' price is a static floor.

**为什么可能错：** Whizz's LA page advertises delivery e-bike rental from $139-169/month ALL-IN (unlimited battery swaps, maintenance, GPS, no credit check) and rent-to-own at $169/mo with ownership in 12 months, directly copying HMP's rent-then-buy funnel; Zoomo prices NYC at $20-24/week. Whizz is VC-funded, already runs DoorDash-co-branded subsidies ($400 off; $80 off first month after 25 deliveries; 15% off with code 15DD), added four DoorDash cities in 2026, and a $139 LA headline below its $169 national price is itself evidence of seeding-mode promotional pricing; if a hub at 768 Ceres starts pulling riders, the rational response is a 2-3 month $99 promo funded from marketing budget, which HMP, carrying $10k/mo rent plus a physical fleet, cannot match without breaking unit economics. The courier's willingness to pay is anchored by the cheapest all-in competitor and by LA earnings (~$150-250/day gross); HMP's standalone battery subscription, the recurring-revenue thesis, is exactly the line Whizz (and Swobbee via DoorDash) bundles for free. A moped premium (speed, range, orders/hour) exists but has not been priced in LA, and SF pricing was set before a $139-169 all-in competitor existed there at scale. Zoomo once called LA a 'top performing market' but its US site now reads NYC-only; a funded operator retreating from LA signals that LA courier e-bike rental economics were not good enough, not an open field.

**方向：** 若错则 LA 更差  ·  **幅度：** 20-40% lower realized rental ARPU per active vehicle (e.g. $55/wk assumed vs $35-40/wk realized on e-bikes; roughly -$40 to -$70 per unit per month) and battery-subscription revenue on rentals collapsing toward $0 because it must be bundled to match Whizz. On 100 active rentals that is $6-9k/month, close to the full base rent; if the e-bike class is more than half the LA fleet plan, rental-fleet payback stretches from ~12 to 18-24 months and the Month-12 trigger is at risk.

**签约前的便宜测试：** Sign up on getwhizz.com as an LA courier and mystery-shop Whizz, Zoomo-style and local LA operators for the true out-the-door weekly price, deposit, theft fee, wait time, pickup address and what 'free maintenance' excludes; find out whether Whizz has a staffed LA hub or drop-ships bikes; call Zoomo to confirm whether an LA hub still operates; compare Whizz LA vs SF Google review counts as a proxy for active riders; check monthly whether LA appears on DoorDash's Dasher Central bike page (Sep 2026 lists NYC, SF, DC, Philadelphia, Chicago, Jersey City, not LA) because that is the moment Whizz gets zero-CAC in-app distribution. Run a 2-week Facebook Marketplace / Craigslist / WhatsApp-group ad in Spanish and English at the intended LA price and count qualified sign-ups; survey 30 couriers at DTLA/Koreatown hotspots on current cost, what they ride and max weekly price.

**需要 HMP 内部数据：** SF realized (not list) weekly rental price net of discounts and free weeks, and active rental count by month for the 12 months before and after Whizz's 2026 DoorDash SF launch; churn of e-bike-class renters vs moped/motorcycle-class renters in that window; SF battery-subscription price and churn; exit-interview reasons mentioning Whizz or price; share of SF renters who switched to a competitor and why.

**可一票否决：** 是  ·  **提出视角：** competition_and_platforms, unit_economics_capital（合并自 2 条）

> 评审（model_output_sensitivity）：Contribution per rental is a thin residual (ARPU ~$240/mo minus $150-200/mo of depreciation, insurance, theft and battery), so break-even count has elasticity ~3-4 to ARPU: a 20-40% realized-price shortfall against Whizz's $139-169 all-in ($55/wk assumed vs $35-40/wk realized, -$40-70 per unit-month) cuts contribution 50-100% and doubles-to-quintuples the rentals needed. On 100 units it removes $6-9k/mo of Month-12 revenue, roughly the whole base rent, and it zeroes the battery-subscription line on rentals because the bundle must match Whizz. A 2x error here ($60 vs $30/wk) makes contribution negative, i.e. no break-even at any count.

> 评审（least_testable_pre_lease）：Mystery-shopping Whizz settles today's $139-169 all-in price, but the assumption that matters is that this is a static floor. A VC-funded competitor's promotional response to a new hub pulling its riders, and the timing of DoorDash flipping LA onto its Dasher Central bike page (zero-CAC distribution for Whizz), cannot be observed until HMP is operating and visible. Realized rental ARPU 20-40% below plan on 100 units is roughly the whole base rent, and the standalone battery subscription is exactly the line the competitor bundles for free. The founder signs while blind to the competitor's second move by construction.

## 3. A07 · 需求测算 · Borda 13

**假设：** DTLA/Koreatown app couriers are mostly on two wheels or readily convertible from cars (implicitly 20-30% of car couriers, because parking is bad), the two-wheelers are e-bike/e-moped riders HMP can serve as in SF (not gas 125-150cc scooter/motorcycle riders or cheap Amazon e-scooter riders), and because LA is spread out they want faster FLASH / Lightning-class vehicles, so a 100+ unit rental fleet weighted toward tiers C/D is addressable near 768 Ceres.

**为什么可能错：** LA app couriers are overwhelmingly car-based: DoorDash reports 72% of SF deliveries on two wheels but only ~30% in San Diego; LA is unpublished and plausibly at or below San Diego, with two-wheeler deliveries growing ~4x faster than car (2024-25) but from a low base. LA car couriers take long-radius orders (platforms route farther, higher-payout orders to cars; a Koreatown car courier delivers to Hollywood, Mid-City, Silver Lake), stack Uber rides / Instacart / Amazon Flex on the same car and use it for family; switching means giving up range and multi-platform income, choosing 'bike/scooter' on Uber Eats restricts the delivery radius, and the parking penalty applies at pickup only while drop-offs are spread far. It is unproven that SF growth came from car converts; SF buyers may have been upgrading from bicycles, gas scooters, cheap e-bikes or foot. Two customers are conflated: HMP's FLASH / Lightning MK.II buyers skew toward commuters and car-replacement buyers who BUY, while couriers who RENT are cash-constrained and choose the cheapest vehicle that clears the app and the law, which in California is a Class 2 e-bike. The two-wheel minority is concentrated in dense pockets (DTLA, K-town, Hollywood, Westwood, Santa Monica/Venice) where a 20 mph e-bike already suffices, which is exactly why those couriers are on two wheels; 'LA is spread out, therefore faster vehicles' has the causality backwards. A car-first rider who does switch needs range and 35-50 mph over longer LA legs, pushing toward Lightning-class vehicles that require license, registration, insurance and an M1/M2, destroying the 'no license' value prop that drove SF sales; the SF installed base is dominated by 20 mph class-2-styled mopeds and there is little SF evidence HMP can sell or rent licensed 40-70 mph vehicles at volume. Whatever two-wheel share exists may also be gas scooters and motorcycles (freeway-legal, 60+ mile range, no charging, bought used on OfferUp) who need no battery subscription, or cheap Amazon e-bikes/e-scooters whose riders will not buy a $3-5k vehicle. The site question and the fleet-mix question are the same question: the right fleet for a Ceres Ave hub may be tier A for DTLA, and the right site for tier A may not be Ceres Ave.

**方向：** 若错则 LA 更差  ·  **幅度：** Addressable two-wheel couriers in DTLA+Koreatown may be 300-800 active riders, not several thousand; at an SF-like 30% share ceiling that is 100-250 vehicles. Realistic 12-month car-to-two-wheel conversion 2-8% instead of 20-30%; if convertible car riders are the majority of proposed SAM, SAM shrinks 50-70%. E-bike/e-moped fraction of observed two-wheelers could be 30-60% rather than the ~90% implied by SF, shrinking battery-subscription SAM (e-motorcycle sales SAM might be modestly larger). If LA two-wheel share is ~20-30% and tier C/D demand within it is <20%, the addressable tier C/D rental pool near Ceres Ave could be in the low dozens of riders versus a plan sized for 100+ rental units, with lower conversion and higher warranty/insurance friction on the licensed share.

**签约前的便宜测试：** Three dinner rushes (Fri/Sat/Sun 6-9pm) of vehicle-type tallies (car / gas scooter / motorcycle / e-bike / e-moped / other; note plates and licensing) at 5-10 LA hotspots (DTLA/Arts District, Koreatown, Westlake, USC, Hollywood, Westwood, Santa Monica) and at 10 SF hotspots at the same hour to compute observed two-wheel share directly. Intercept-survey 50 car couriers at 3 Koreatown pickup hotspots: prior vehicle, apps used, typical order radius, whether they would switch and what would make them. Check Uber Eats / DoorDash LA sign-up flows for what vehicle choice does to assignment radius and whether 'bike/scooter' onboarding is waitlisted (a demand signal). Ask DoorDash's LA city team for LA two-wheel share (they publish it for other cities). Look at Whizz LA and Zoomo LA fleet type and pricing (20-28 mph e-bikes, implying where LA two-wheel demand sits). Count LA OfferUp/Craigslist 'delivery' scooter listings by type. Pull HMP's Shopify orders shipped to LA County zips by model and email those buyers a 3-question survey (courier vs commuter, would you rent, which tier).

**需要 HMP 内部数据：** Prior vehicle of SF buyers (car / gas scooter / cheap e-bike / bicycle / none; if fewer than ~15% were car converts, exclude car riders from LA SAM); SF sales mix by model and speed class (FLASH vs Lightning vs SWFT vs e-bike) 2022-2026; share of FLASH / Lightning MK.II buyers who are delivery riders vs commuters/enthusiasts; share of SF couriers who rent tier A vs any faster tier; SF renter-to-buyer conversion by tier; SF rental waitlist by vehicle type; return/complaint rate on licensed vehicles; average daily miles per rider from battery/GPS data; battery-subscription attach rate by product line; Shopify sales to LA County zips by model and buyer type.

**可一票否决：** 是  ·  **提出视角：** demand_sizing_method, sf_to_la_transferability, regulation_and_fleet_mix（合并自 4 条）

> 评审（model_output_sensitivity）：Two multiplicative SAM factors with independent 2-3x errors: (1) two-wheel share and convertibility (LA plausibly at or below San Diego's ~30% two-wheel share; car-to-two-wheel conversion 2-8% not 20-30%, so SAM -50-70% if car converts are the majority of proposed SAM) and (2) the HMP-serviceable fraction of observed two-wheelers (e-bike/e-moped 30-60% rather than ~90%, shrinking battery SAM). It also drives the fleet-mix module: the tier C/D rental pool near Ceres may be in the low dozens versus a plan for 100+ units, so $200-400k of tier C/D fleet capex is sized to a pool that may not exist. Addressable riders 300-800 rather than several thousand; at a 30% share cap that is 100-250 vehicles, a 2-4x change in SOM.

> 评审（least_testable_pre_lease）：The observed two-wheel share and e-bike vs gas mix at LA hotspots is a three-evening tally, so the current addressable pool is testable and the plan should be sized on it. The residuals are not: whether car couriers convert at 20-30% or 2-8%, and whether tier C/D (licensed, 40-70 mph) vehicles actually get rented at volume by cash-constrained couriers rather than sitting at 30-50% utilization while tier A sells out. That is a fleet-capex shape decision (tens of units at $4-6k each) that must be made before opening and is only validated by months of rental uptake; A11's license survey partially bounds it, which is why it ranks here and not higher.

> 评审（probability_of_being_wrong）：The only hard datum on LA-type markets points the wrong way: DoorDash publishes 72% two-wheel in SF but ~30% in San Diego, and LA (unpublished) is a car-default metro where couriers stack Uber/Instacart/Flex on one car and platforms route long-radius orders to cars. Tried to argue it is true (LA has dense pockets, two-wheel growing 4x faster) and failed: the pockets that already exist are where a 20 mph e-bike suffices, so 'LA is spread out, therefore faster tiers' has the causality backwards, and Zoomo's retreat to NYC-only after once calling LA a top market is consistent with a thin two-wheel courier base. Consequence: SAM shrinks 50-70% and the fleet mix is tilted toward the wrong tiers; this is the denominator every other number sits on.

## 4. A15 · 单位经济 · Borda 12

**假设：** Theft/non-return, bad debt, crash damage and insurance total ~3-5% of fleet value per year, scale only modestly with vehicle speed, and are neutralized by ~$15-30k of gate, cameras, alarm, indoor storage and vehicle GPS at the depot, so per-unit contribution margin holds across tiers even with vehicles used on-street 10+ hours a day and 100-300 packs stored on the edge of Skid Row.

## 13. A13 · 法规/车型 · Borda 1
